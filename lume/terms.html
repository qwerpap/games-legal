What Lume is

Lume suggests what a wristwatch might be from a photograph, and gives you the checks — the engraved numbers, the calibre, the case width, the bezel — that narrow the list down. It is an aid for owners and collectors.

It is not an authority, and it never judges authenticity. A photograph cannot separate many watches from one another, and it certainly cannot tell a genuine watch from a good copy — so Lume does not try. It answers with a shortlist and a confidence rather than a verdict. Do not rely on it for a purchase, a sale, an insurance valuation or a dispute. For that, a watchmaker or an authorised dealer opens the case.

Value ranges

Each entry stores one range — what an excellent, running example tends to fetch on the pre-owned market — and the app scales it for the condition you pick, showing the multiplier it used. Ranges are rough guides that go out of date. Condition, service history, box and papers, and the market itself move a real price far more than the model name does. Nothing in Lume is an appraisal, a valuation or an offer.

Looking after the watch

Everything Lume asks you to check is on the outside. Do not lever the case back off to read a number: it breaks the seal, and on a screw-down back it scars the metal. If a marking is only visible inside, a watchmaker opens it.
